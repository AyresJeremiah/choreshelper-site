Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-10-11

In short: ChoresHelper collects nothing for the developer. Your household's data lives on a server that you run yourself.

Who this applies to

This policy covers the ChoresHelper iPhone and iPad app and the ChoresHelper server software. ChoresHelper is self-hosted: each household runs its own server. There is no ChoresHelper-operated cloud service, and the developer has no access to your server or your data.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app and the server software contain no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDKs. Neither sends any information to the developer or to any third party. The app's App Store privacy label is "Data Not Collected".

Where your data lives

All household data is stored on the server your household runs, and only your household's administrator controls it. The app connects directly to that server over your own network. The server's operator is responsible for its security, backups and updates.

What is stored on your server

  • Household member names.
  • Chores, schedules and the history of completed chores.
  • Allowance records.
  • Login accounts: a username and a password stored only as a hash, plus sign-in tokens.

This data is created by your household and is visible to the people your administrator has given access to.

Location

If you choose to allow it, the app uses your device's location only to detect that you have arrived home, so it can show a reminder. This is processed on your device. Your location is never sent to your server, to the developer or to anyone else. The app works without location access; reminders then fall back to times your household sets.

Notifications

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device. ChoresHelper does not use Apple's push notification service or any other push service, and no notification content passes through any third party.

Local network

The app uses your local network only to find and connect to your household's own server.

Account deletion

A household administrator can delete any login account, and you can delete your own account in the app under Settings, Account, Delete account (you confirm with your password). Deleting an account deletes that person's login and password and signs out all of their devices by revoking their sign-in tokens. The household member and their chore and allowance history are kept on the household's server so that the household's records remain intact; an administrator can later remove the member from the household. A household's last administrator account cannot be deleted; another administrator must be made first.

Because all data is on the household's own server, an administrator can also delete data directly, or remove everything by deleting the server's storage.

Children's privacy

ChoresHelper is designed to be used by families, including children. The developer collects no information from anyone, children included. The household administrator, typically a parent, creates children's accounts and controls all of the data on the household's server.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
